City-Wide ANPR, Vehicle Trajectory Reconstruction & GIS Traffic Analytics

A distributed event-processing system that turns city CCTV into standardized plate observations, reconstructs vehicle routes across geographically separated cameras, and visualizes congestion, origin–destination flow, and real-time alerts on a GIS command center.

ANPR > 90% OCR target Probabilistic trajectory engine PostGIS city map Real-time alerts Scalable from 10 → 100+ cameras

Core insight

Do not track every vehicle from raw CCTV

Each camera emits a standardized observation. The central system connects those events into a route.

Impact and benefits

Why this matters for a city and for BEL

01

Operational command

A live city picture: vehicle counts, average speed, congestion heatmaps, and route popularity from the same event stream.

02

Public safety & enforcement

Watch-listed plates, stolen vehicles, and unusual hops across the camera graph can raise alerts without continuous video tracking.

03

Planning intelligence

Origin–destination matrices, peak-hour demand, and travel-time reliability support signal timing and corridor upgrades.

04

Reuse of existing CCTV

Edge ANPR upgrades existing cameras instead of installing a new tracking sensor on every road segment.

05

Privacy-aware by design

The system stores structured observations, not endless raw video streams, reducing bandwidth and retention risk.

06

Strategic fit for BEL

Matches a defence-electronics brief: distributed sensing, resilient analytics, GIS visualization, and city-scale operations.

Research and references

Foundations the design is built on

ANPR / ALPR literature

YOLO-class detectors for vehicles and plates; two-stage pipelines (detect → enhance → OCR). PaddleOCR and dedicated ANPR OCR for Indian plates, including multi-line and regional formats.

Multi-camera tracking

Re-identification across non-overlapping views is a classic computer-vision problem. Plate text is a strong ID, but OCR noise requires fuzzy matching and temporal-spatial gating.

Network / GIS traffic science

Road graphs, expected travel times, and OD matrices come from transport engineering. PostGIS shortest-path and map-matching constrain impossible hops.

Event streaming practice

Kafka-style event buses, ClickHouse analytics, and GIS frontends (MapLibre / OpenLayers) are established patterns for city operations platforms.

Reference themes to cite in a write-up

Automatic Number Plate Recognition surveys · YOLO object detection papers · PaddleOCR / CRNN OCR · Multi-target multi-camera tracking (MTMCT) · Map-matching and HMM trajectory reconstruction · Urban OD estimation from probe / camera data · Indian ITS and smart-city camera deployments.

Feasibility and viability

Hard problem, staged so it can ship

What is already viable

Vehicle and plate detection, OCR above 90% in good lighting, event ingestion, PostGIS mapping, and a React command dashboard are all implementable with current open-source stacks.

The real difficulty

OCR is not the hardest part. The hard part is deciding whether two observations from distant cameras belong to the same vehicle and reconstructing a reliable route.

Risks to absorb

OCR confusions: 8 ↔ B, 0 ↔ O
Missed detections and camera outages
Duplicates, similar plates, direction flips
Variable speeds and route changes

Why a staged MVP is viable

10 cameras→ ANPR→ Central server→ PostGIS→ A→B→C→D demo

Prove one vehicle across four cameras. Then scale to 100 cameras, city-wide reconstruction, analytics, and alerts. Architecture stays the same.
